## tinypdf

[0:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aUHVOcsnRQ&t=19)

- https://github.com/Lulzx/tinypdf

Tiny PDF is a minimal PDF creation library that does one thing extremely well. This entire library is under 400 lines of code, weighs just 3.3 kilobytes, has zero dependencies, and still generates real standards compliant PDFs. Compared to something like JSPDF, Tiny PDF is nearly 70 times smaller because it intentionally cuts everything you don't need, just the 95% use case, putting text and images on a page.

## Qwen-Image-Layered

[1:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aUHVOcsnRQ&t=72)

- https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image-Layered

Quen image layered, a really exciting project pushing image editing in a much more fundamental direction. Instead of treating an image as a single flat output, Quinn image layered can decompose an image into multiple RGBA layers. Similar to how designers work in tools like Photoshop or Figma, this layered representation gives you inherent editability. Each layer can be resized, repositioned, or recolored independently.

## ovim

[1:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aUHVOcsnRQ&t=99)

- https://github.com/tonisives/ovim

OIM, a Mac OS tool that brings systemwide Vim key bindings to every app on your machine. Oim gives you a true modal editing experience across Mac OS and it does this using two powerful modes depending on how deep you want to go. It intercepts your keystrokes and translates Vim motions into native Mac OS key events so you get instant modal navigation and editing inside any app.

## Lightron

[2:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aUHVOcsnRQ&t=124)

- https://github.com/lwj2015/lightron

Lightnutried training framework for large language models. Lightron is designed to sit right in the sweet spot between tiny educational implementations and full-blown production scale systems. It's clean and modern without sacrificing serious distributed capabilities. Lightron supports full 4D parallelism, tensor parallelism, pipeline parallelism, data parallelism, and context parallelism, all in under 1,000 lines of core code. Ever wish you could talk to the past?

## History LLMs

[2:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aUHVOcsnRQ&t=154)

- https://github.com/DGoettlich/history-llms

Meet History LLMs, timelocked language models trained entirely on historical texts. Ask about 1913 or 1946, and get answers as people of that era actually thought. Unlike modern AI, these models don't roleplay. They embody the textual culture of their time, from newspapers to novels. Curious what educated Europeans worried about before WWI? History LLM will tell you in their own words.

## Fuzzy Canary

[3:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aUHVOcsnRQ&t=183)

- https://github.com/vivienhenz24/fuzzy-canary

AI companies are scraping everyone's blogs for training data. And if you're self-hosting, you basically have no defense. That's where Fuzzy Canary comes in. It's a poor man's anti-scraping trick that plants invisible links in your HTML. Links that trigger AI companies content safety filters. Yeah, those kinds of sites. Scrapers see the page, panic, and hopefully drop your content. Just install it, add one line, and you're done.

## DVGT

[7:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aUHVOcsnRQ&t=427)

- https://github.com/wzzheng/DVGT

DVGT short for driving visual geometry transformer. A universal framework for autonomous driving perception. ## Particulate

[7:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aUHVOcsnRQ&t=453)

- https://github.com/RuiningLi/particulate

Particulate, a feed forward system for 3D object articulation. The model is ultra fast, recovering a fully articulated 3D object in about 10 seconds with just a single forward pass.

## Gazetteer

Gaziteer is a fully offline boundarybased reverse geocoding library written in Python.
