What is up developers? Welcome back to GitHub awesome. Today we're looking at 35 self-hosted projects on GitHub. Let's get into it.

## tududi

[0:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=10)

- https://github.com/chrisvel/tududi

Tudoist and Things are polished tools that store your entire life on someone else's servers and charge monthly for the privilege. Tudy is the self-hosted alternative built around a different philosophy entirely. Tasks, projects, notes, and areas with smart workflows tying them together running on your own server. No gamified streaks, no notification badges, no anxietyinducing UI, just a quiet place to think and plan.

## LeafWiki

[0:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=38)

- https://github.com/perber/leafwiki

Setting up a self-hosted wiki shouldn't mean inheriting another complex production stack to monitor. Leaf wiki is built specifically to keep your engineering docs simple and independent. It packages a clean web interface as a single Go binary with zero external database dependencies. It stores all your content directly on disk as plain markdown files, making backups as easy as a standard folder copy.

## LinguaCafe

[1:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=64)

- https://github.com/simjanos-dev/LinguaCafe

Many developers build their best side projects simply because the commercial alternatives are way too expensive or packed with useless features. Lingua Cafe is a self-hosted language learning platform born from that exact frustration. It lets you import ebooks and YouTube subtitles to read in your target language. The app automatically parses words and schedules spaced repetition reviews, keeping your study logs fully local.

## open-notebook

[1:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=92)

- https://github.com/lfnovo/open-notebook

If you love Google's Notebook LM, but hate sending private research papers straight to big tech servers, you need to check out Open Notebook. It is a self-hosted alternative that runs on your own hardware using Docker. You can ingest PDFs or web pages, then chat with your data using local models through Olama. It gives you a complete REST API and even generates custom podcasts with up to four speakers.

## many-notes

[1:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=118)

- https://github.com/brufdev/many-notes

Obsidian and notion keep piling on features until notetaking feels like a second job. Many notes goes the other direction on purpose. It's a markdown note-taking web app designed around one idea, simplicity. Organize your notes in vaults. Write in plain markdown. And that's largely the point. No plug-in marketplace to manage. No database lockin, just clean, fast notes you self-host and own.

## ignis

[2:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=143)

- https://github.com/Nystik-gh/ignis

Your full Obsidian setup, plugins and all, running on your own server, accessible from any browser anywhere. Your notes, your plugins, your infrastructure. No Obsidian Sync subscription required.

## scratch

[2:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=168)

- https://github.com/erictli/scratch

Offline first means it works instantly with no connection and your notes live on your device.

## OmniTools

[3:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=219)

## lupine

[5:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=354)

- https://github.com/lupinemachines/lupine

You've got a beefy GPU in your gaming rig and a CPUon home server that desperately wants it for AI workloads. Normally, that GPU is trapped in one box. Lupine breaks it loose. ## trailbase

[6:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=381)

- https://github.com/trailbaseio/trailbase

Firebase locks you in. Superbase self-hosted means running Postgress plus a stack of services. Trailbase strips it down to a single executable. A complete Firebase alternative in one file.

## Desomnia

[6:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lPZrLtM3JBI&t=406)

- https://github.com/mad0x20wizard/Desomnia

Running a home server means choosing between wasting electricity 24/7 or manually managing sleep and wake cycles. Dsomnia is a declarative network aware sleep manager that handles it automatically. It monitors active user sessions and open SMB shares, sends the host to sleep once everything goes quiet, then intercepts incoming connections and transparently wakes the server on demand.
