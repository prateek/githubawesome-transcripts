## CommonForms

[0:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=peJr-6BRQ3M&t=23)

- https://github.com/jbarrow/commonforms

First up, Common Forms, a project that automatically converts any PDF into a fillable form. This repo introduces a data set for form field detection, turning document understanding into an object detection problem. The team started with 8 million PDFs, filtered them down to 55,000 documents covering over 450,000 pages in multiple languages and domains. What's cool is that they didn't just stop at data. They also released FFDNet, a pair of open-source models that detecting text inputs, checkboxes, and signature fields with precision.

## TinyRecursiveModels

[0:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=peJr-6BRQ3M&t=59)

- https://github.com/SamsungSAILMontreal/TinyRecursiveModels

Next up, meet tiny recursive models or TRM. Proof that in AI, less really is more. This open source project explores recursive reasoning with small neural networks. We're talking just 7 million parameters. Yet, it scores 45% on ARC AGI1 and 8% on ARC AGI 2, outperforming models thousands of times larger. The magic tr doesn't rely on massive data or billion-dollar GPUs. Instead, it recurses on itself, refining its own answers over multiple reasoning steps. A minimalist approach that's rewriting how we think about intelligence in small models.

## Dexter

[2:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=peJr-6BRQ3M&t=133)

- https://github.com/virattt/dexter

Next on our list is Dexter, an open-source financial agent in just about 200 lines of code. Imagine clawed code, but built specifically for financial analysis. Dexter plans tasks, runs autonomously, validates its own work, and even researches stocks, all using real-time market data. It's powered by open tools like Langchain for managing LLM, prompts, and outputs. And the best part, you don't need coding experience to get started. Under the hood, Dexter breaks complex financial questions into step-by-step research plans, executes them, and refineses the results until it's confident in its findings.

## Puffin

[5:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=peJr-6BRQ3M&t=359)

- https://github.com/KangLiao929/Puffin

Meet Puff and Thinking, a multimodal model that integrates camera geometry directly into its neural reasoning. This model doesn't just process images. ## Cronboard

[6:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=peJr-6BRQ3M&t=395)

- https://github.com/antoniorodr/Cronboard

If you've ever lost track of your scheduled jobs, meet your command line best friend, Crawboard. Crawboard is a terminal based application that gives you a clean, human readable view of all your cron jobs, both local and remote. It's built for system administrators, DevOps engineers, and anyone who lives inside a terminal window. With Crawboard, you can add, edit, pause, or delete cron jobs in seconds. No more manually editing cron tabs and hoping you don't break syntax. It connects securely via SSH, supports both password and key authentication, and even shows last and next runtimes in a beautifully formatted way.

## Pyversity

[7:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=peJr-6BRQ3M&t=434)

- https://github.com/Pringled/pyversity

Ever noticed how search results often look exactly the same at the top? Enter Pyiversity, a lightweight Python library designed to make retrieval results more diverse without losing relevance. Pyiversity implements popular strategies like MMR, MSD, DPP, and cover to efficiently rerank results, ensuring you get items that are both relevant and varied. The API is simple, fast, and only depends on NumPy. So even thousands of results can be diversified in milliseconds. Whether it's e-commerce, news search, academic retrieval, or feeding LLMs with non-redundant passages, Pyiversity helps you explore more and repeat less.

## Blaze

[8:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=peJr-6BRQ3M&t=511)

- https://github.com/wizenheimer/blaze

Next up is Blaze, a tiny hackable full text search engine built for hackers, not hyperscalers. Blaze implements inverted indexing, boolean queries, phrase search, proximity ranking, and BM25 scoring, all in a small, understandable Go code base. If you've ever wondered how search engines work under the hood, Blaze lets you explore tokenization, stemming, stop word filtering, and index structures, all without the overhead of massive systems. It's thread safe, fast, and serializable, making it perfect for learning, prototyping, or building smallcale search apps.

## Keyer

[9:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=peJr-6BRQ3M&t=547)

- https://github.com/mafik/keyer

Meet Kier, a one-handed, fully customizable keyboard that lets you type with minimal finger movement, freeing your other hand to sip tea or do literally anything else. Using just 10 mechanical keys, Kier supports hundreds of chords, arpeggios, and multi-layer shortcuts. Enough to type efficiently, even if you lose a finger. The magic is in its DIY moldable design. You build a metal wire skeleton, cover it with modeling clay, attach key switches, and flash the firmware. The end result is a keyboard that literally fits your hand.

And if you're feeling adventurous, Kier can even be paired with smart glasses or sensors to control your computer without touching it.
